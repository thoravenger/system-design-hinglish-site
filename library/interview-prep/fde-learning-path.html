Forward Deployed Engineer

A route from wherever you are now to a job where you sit down with a customer, work out what they actually need, and ship it before you leave.

Six phases. Tick things off as you go and the rail on the left fills in behind you.

Written assuming you can already write some code and have around ten hours a week. If neither is true, Phase 1 gets longer and nothing else changes. None of this is a course you buy — it's the list of things you have to be able to do.

Things to skip

Every hour spent here is an hour not spent on the list above.

  • Grinding algorithm puzzles past the basics. There is a coding bar and you have to clear it, but nobody has been hired into this role for being quick at dynamic programming.
  • Collecting courses. Two is plenty. The fifth one is procrastination wearing a lanyard.
  • Fine-tuning. Not until you have exhausted prompting, retrieval and evaluation, which almost nobody has.
  • Another chatbot with no evaluation. Every applicant has one. It reads as a tutorial you finished, not a system you own.
  • Waiting until you feel ready to talk to a real user. Do it in week two, badly.
  • Kubernetes, unless a job you actually want names it.

Phases 1 to 3 are partly skippable — take only what you don't already have. Phase 4 isn't really a phase, it's a habit, so start it in week one and never stop. Phase 5 is the only one a hiring manager can actually see.

One test for whether you're ready: can you take a vague sentence from someone non-technical, turn it into a written scope by Friday, and have something running they can click by the end of the second week?
