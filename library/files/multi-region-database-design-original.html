Why two regions?

Do regions ki zaroorat kyun, aur mushkil kya hai

Problem

ShopKart's only database lives in Mumbai. US users (30%) pay about 190 ms extra on every database round trip. A fire, flood or power failure in that one region takes the whole business down. And some countries want their citizens' data kept inside the country. The moment you add a second region 12,854 km away, you hit the one limit no money can buy past: the speed of light.

ShopKart ka ek hi database Mumbai mein hai. US ke users (30%) ko har database round trip pe lagbhag 190 ms extra lagte hain. Us ek region mein aag, baadh ya power failure poora business band kar deti hai. Aur kuch desh chahte hain ki unke nagrikon ka data desh ke andar hi rahe. Jaise hi 12,854 km door doosra region jodte ho, woh limit saamne aati hai jise paise se paar nahi kar sakte: light ki speed.

Solution

Three reasons to run in two regions

  1. Latency: serve each user from a nearby copy, above all for reads.Latency: har user ko paas wali copy se serve karo, khaas taur pe reads.
  2. Disaster recovery: survive the loss of a whole region with a known data-loss window (RPO) and a known recovery time (RTO).Disaster recovery: poora region chala jaaye tab bhi bacho, ek tay data-loss window (RPO) aur tay recovery time (RTO) ke saath.
  3. Data residency: keep a country's users' data inside that country when the law asks for it.Data residency: jab kanoon maange, kisi desh ke users ka data usi desh mein rakho.

The trade you cannot avoid

Every write must choose. Either it waits for the other region to confirm (safe, but +190 ms), or it does not wait (fast, but the other region is a little behind, and a disaster can lose the newest writes). Every design on this page is a way of making that choice per piece of data.

Har write ko chunna padta hai. Ya toh woh doosre region ki confirmation ka intezaar kare (safe, par +190 ms), ya intezaar na kare (tez, par doosra region thoda peeche rehta hai, aur disaster mein sabse naye writes kho sakte hain). Is page ka har design, data ke har hisse ke liye yahi choice karne ka ek tareeka hai.

CAP and PACELC in one line: if the link between regions breaks, you choose consistency or availability (CAP). Even when nothing is broken, you still choose latency or consistency (PACELC). Topic 15 animates both choices.

CAP aur PACELC ek line mein: regions ke beech ka link toote toh consistency ya availability mein se ek chuno (CAP). Kuch na toote tab bhi latency ya consistency mein se ek chunna padta hai (PACELC). Topic 15 mein dono choices ka animation hai.

Say this in the interview: "Two regions are not only about speed. They force me to decide which data can be a little old and which data must never be lost."

Interview mein bolo: "Do regions sirf speed ke liye nahi hain. Yeh mujhe tay karne pe majboor karte hain ki kaunsa data thoda purana chal sakta hai aur kaunsa kabhi khona nahi chahiye."

Replication 101

Sync, async, semi-sync: write "done" kab maana jaaye

Problem

Replication keeps copies of the same data on several machines. But when is a write "done": when the first machine saves it, or when the far copy has it too? That one choice decides speed, how much data you can lose, and what happens when the network breaks.

Replication same data ki copies kai machines pe rakhta hai. Par write "done" kab hai: jab pehli machine save kare, ya jab door wali copy ke paas bhi aa jaaye? Yahi ek choice speed, kitna data kho sakta hai, aur network tootne pe kya hoga, sab tay karti hai.

Solution

In a single-leader database the leader appends every change to a log (the WAL in Postgres, the binlog in MySQL). Followers stream that log and replay it in the same order, so they end up with the same data.

Single-leader database mein leader har change ko ek log mein likhta hai (Postgres mein WAL, MySQL mein binlog). Followers woh log stream karke usi order mein replay karte hain, isliye unke paas same data aa jaata hai.

ModeClient hears "OK" afterExtra latencyData at risk
Asynchronousthe leader's own commitnonewrites not yet shipped
Semi-synchronousone replica has received it1 round tripalmost none
Synchronousa replica has made it durable1 round trip + disk flushnone

The usual mix: synchronous inside a region (a standby in another zone, about 1–2 ms away) and asynchronous across regions (190 ms away). You get zero data loss for a machine or zone failure, and only a small window of loss for a whole-region disaster.

Aam mix: region ke andar synchronous (doosre zone mein standby, lagbhag 1–2 ms door) aur regions ke beech asynchronous (190 ms door). Machine ya zone fail ho toh zero data loss, aur poore region ki disaster mein sirf chhoti si loss window.

SQL vs NoSQL

SQL: MySQL / Postgres

  • One leader per shard takes all writes; followers are read-only.Har shard ka ek leader saare writes leta hai; followers sirf read ke liye.
  • Replication is log shipping: Postgres streaming replication, MySQL binlog with GTIDs.Replication matlab log shipping: Postgres streaming replication, MySQL binlog GTIDs ke saath.
  • You choose the mode: Postgres synchronous_commit, MySQL semi-sync.Mode aap chunte ho: Postgres synchronous_commit, MySQL semi-sync.
  • Failover means promoting a follower to leader.Failover matlab kisi follower ko leader banana.

NoSQL: Cassandra / DynamoDB

  • Cassandra has no leader: any replica accepts a write, and the client decides how many must answer (the consistency level).Cassandra mein koi leader nahi: koi bhi replica write le sakta hai, aur client tay karta hai kitne replicas jawab dein (consistency level).
  • Replicas are placed per data centre, for example 3 in Mumbai and 3 in Virginia.Replicas har data centre ke hisaab se rakhe jaate hain, jaise 3 Mumbai mein aur 3 Virginia mein.
  • DynamoDB keeps copies across zones inside a region for you; global tables replicate between regions, and every region can take writes.DynamoDB region ke andar zones mein copies khud rakhta hai; global tables regions ke beech replicate karti hain, aur har region writes le sakta hai.
  • No failover step: clients simply use the other region's replicas.Koi failover step nahi: clients bas doosre region ke replicas use karte hain.

Replication is not a backup. A bad DELETE replicates to every copy in milliseconds. Keep point-in-time backups as well.

Replication backup nahi hai. Galat DELETE milliseconds mein har copy tak pahunch jaata hai. Point-in-time backups alag se rakho.

Distance becomes latency

Doori kaise latency ban jaati hai, aur sync ki keemat

Problem

The database commits in 2 ms, yet a write from Virginia to a Mumbai leader takes about 190 ms. Where does the time go? And if cross-region replication is made synchronous, what does that cost for one write and for the whole system?

Database 2 ms mein commit karta hai, phir bhi Virginia se Mumbai leader tak ka write lagbhag 190 ms leta hai. Time kahan jaata hai? Aur agar cross-region replication synchronous kar dein, toh ek write aur poore system pe kya keemat padegi?

Solution

Distancegreat circle, Mumbai to N. Virginia12,854 km
Light in fibreabout 2/3 of its speed in vacuum200 km per ms
Physics minimum round trip2 × 12,854 ÷ 200128.5 ms
Measured round tripcables are not straight, plus routers≈ 190 ms
Async commitwait for the local commit only2 ms
Sync commit to Virginia2 + 190192 ms
Commits in flight at peakLittle's Law: 6,944 × 0.192 s1,333
Chatty transaction from the US5 separate round trips × 190950 ms
  • 128.5 ms is physics. No hardware beats it. Plan with the measured number, and measure it between your own regions.128.5 ms physics hai. Koi hardware isse tez nahi. Plan measured number se karo, aur apne regions ke beech khud measure karo.
  • Sync across regions multiplies concurrency. 1,333 commits wait at the same moment, so 1,333 connections and threads sit blocked. Group commit batches many transactions into one flush and one round trip, which is how sync replication survives at all.Regions ke beech sync concurrency ko guna kar deta hai. Ek hi pal mein 1,333 commits wait karte hain, yaani 1,333 connections aur threads atke rehte hain. Group commit kai transactions ko ek flush aur ek round trip mein bhejta hai, isi se sync replication tik paata hai.
  • Chatty is deadly. Five statements sent one by one cross the ocean five times. Send one batch, or run the whole request in the leader's region.Baar-baar ki baatcheet jaanleva hai. Ek-ek karke bheje gaye paanch statements paanch baar ocean paar karte hain. Ek batch bhejo, ya poori request leader ke region mein chalao.

The one-second checkout: a US checkout that runs five separate queries against a Mumbai database spends almost a second on the ocean before any business logic runs.

Ek second ka checkout: US ka checkout jo Mumbai database pe paanch alag queries chalata hai, koi business logic chalne se pehle hi lagbhag ek second ocean pe bita deta hai.

Bandwidth, lag and data at risk

Kitna data ocean paar jaata hai, copy kitni peeche, kitna khatre mein

Problem

Async replication is fast, but the far copy is always a little behind. How much data crosses the ocean, how far behind can the copy fall, and how many committed writes disappear if Mumbai goes dark right now?

Async replication tez hai, par door wali copy hamesha thoda peeche rehti hai. Ocean ke paar kitna data jaata hai, copy kitni peeche reh sakti hai, aur agar abhi Mumbai band ho jaaye toh kitne committed writes gayab ho jaayenge?

Solution

Log volume at peak6,944 writes/s × 2 KB × 8111 Mbps
Log volume per day200M × 2 KB, about $8 at an illustrative $0.02/GB400 GB
Normal lagone-way trip + replay time≈ 95 ms + a few ms
Replica slower than leader(6,944 − 5,000) ÷ 5,000+0.39 s lag every second
Catching up100,000 behind ÷ (10,000 − 6,944) spare per s33 s
Data at risk with 1 s of lag6,944 writes/s × 1 s≈ 6,945 writes
  • Bandwidth is rarely the limit; replay speed is. Postgres replays WAL with a single process on the replica, while the leader writes with many connections. MySQL can replay in parallel with multiple replica workers.Bandwidth kam hi limit banti hai; replay speed banti hai. Postgres replica pe WAL ek hi process se replay karta hai, jabki leader kai connections se likhta hai. MySQL kai replica workers se parallel replay kar sakta hai.
  • Big transactions stall replicas. A 10-minute batch UPDATE ships and replays as one unit. Keep transactions small and throttle batch jobs while lag is high.Bade transactions replicas ko rok dete hain. 10 minute ka batch UPDATE ek unit ki tarah jaata aur replay hota hai. Transactions chhote rakho aur lag zyada ho toh batch jobs dheemi karo.
  • Watch lag in seconds and its trend. A lag of 2 s that is falling is fine; a lag of 0.5 s that grows every second is an incident on its way.Lag ko seconds mein aur uske trend ke saath dekho. 2 s ka lag jo ghat raha hai theek hai; 0.5 s ka lag jo har second badh raha hai, aane wala incident hai.

SQL vs NoSQL

SQL: MySQL / Postgres

  • One ordered log per shard; lag is one clear number (bytes or seconds behind).Har shard ka ek ordered log; lag ek saaf number hai (bytes ya seconds peeche).
  • Measure with pg_stat_replication on Postgres or the replica status on MySQL.Postgres pe pg_stat_replication ya MySQL pe replica status se measure karo.

NoSQL: Cassandra / DynamoDB

  • Cassandra sends each write to all replicas at once; the far data centre gets it asynchronously, and hinted handoff plus repair fill any gaps.Cassandra har write saare replicas ko ek saath bhejta hai; door ka data centre use async milta hai, aur hinted handoff aur repair gaps bharte hain.
  • DynamoDB global tables replicate asynchronously; watch the ReplicationLatency metric.DynamoDB global tables async replicate karti hain; ReplicationLatency metric pe nazar rakho.

How many shards and replicas

Kitne shards, har shard ki kitni copies, aur kyun

Problem

One MySQL or Postgres server cannot hold 50 TB, and one leader cannot take unlimited writes. How many shards do we need, how many copies of each, and why not simply buy the biggest machine?

Ek MySQL ya Postgres server 50 TB nahi rakh sakta, aur ek leader unlimited writes nahi le sakta. Kitne shards chahiye, har shard ki kitni copies, aur sabse bada machine hi kyun na le lein?

Solution

Shards by storage⌈49.8 ÷ 1 TB per shard⌉50
Shards by writes⌈6,944 ÷ (4,000 × 0.6)⌉3
Required shardsstorage-boundmax(50, 3)50
Rounded to a power of twoclean halving on every split64
Logical buckets per shard1,024 ÷ 6416
Reads per shard at peak69,444 ÷ 64; one replica does 6,000 at 60%1,085/s
Copies per shardleader + standby in another zone + replica in the other region3
Database nodes64 × 3192
  • Why 1 TB per shard? Rebuilding a lost replica copies the whole shard: 1 TB at 250 MB/s takes about 67 minutes, while a 10 TB shard takes 11 hours of running with one copy fewer. Backups, schema changes and index builds all grow with shard size too.1 TB per shard kyun? Khoya hua replica dobara banane mein poora shard copy hota hai: 1 TB 250 MB/s pe lagbhag 67 minute leta hai, jabki 10 TB shard 11 ghante tak ek copy kam ke saath chalata hai. Backups, schema changes aur index builds bhi shard size ke saath badhte hain.
  • Reads are not the limit here. Each shard sees about 1,085 reads/s and one replica handles 6,000, so replicas exist for failover and for local reads in the far region.Yahan reads limit nahi hain. Har shard pe lagbhag 1,085 reads/s aate hain aur ek replica 6,000 sambhal leta hai, isliye replicas failover aur door wale region mein local reads ke liye hain.
  • Why not one giant machine? It has a hard ceiling, one leader is still one failure domain, and a 50 TB restore would take days.Ek bahut bada machine kyun nahi? Uski hard limit hai, ek leader ab bhi ek failure domain hai, aur 50 TB ka restore dino mein hoga.

SQL vs NoSQL

SQL: MySQL / Postgres

  • You size and create shards yourself, or with middleware such as Vitess (MySQL) or Citus (Postgres).Shards aap khud size karke banate ho, ya Vitess (MySQL) ya Citus (Postgres) jaise middleware se.

Which region is the master?

Door wale server mein se master kaun bane

Problem

Mumbai and Virginia both have users and both hold a copy. In a single-leader SQL database only one of them may take writes for a given row. Which one should be the master, and what if the answer is wrong for a third of your users?

Mumbai aur Virginia dono mein users hain aur dono ke paas copy hai. Single-leader SQL database mein kisi row ke writes sirf ek hi le sakta hai. Master kaun bane, aur agar jawab ek-tihaai users ke liye galat ho toh?

Solution

Decide in this order

  1. Law first. If residency rules say Indian users' data stays in India, their leader must be in Mumbai, whatever latency says.Pehle kanoon. Agar residency rules kehte hain ki Indian users ka data India mein rahe, toh unka leader Mumbai mein hi hoga, latency kuch bhi kahe.
  2. Follow the writes. Put the leader where most writes come from, weighted by how latency-sensitive they are.Writes ke peeche chalo. Leader wahan rakho jahan se zyada writes aate hain, is hisaab se ki woh latency ke liye kitne sensitive hain.
  3. Keep writers next to the leader. The app servers that write must run in the leader's region, never chatting across the ocean.Likhne walon ko leader ke paas rakho. Jo app servers likhte hain woh leader ke region mein chalein, ocean ke paar baatcheet kabhi nahi.
Where the leaders liveIndia writes (70%)US writes (30%)Average per write
All in Mumbai2 ms190 ms0.7 × 2 + 0.3 × 190 = 58.4 ms
All in Virginia190 ms2 ms133.6 ms
Each user's home region2 ms2 ms≈ 2 ms

If you must pick one master, pick Mumbai. But every US write still pays 190 ms, and a checkout with several writes feels it. When that is unacceptable, or when residency rules apply, give each user a home region: the shard key starts with the region, and each shard's leader sits where its users are.

Agar ek hi master chunna hai, toh Mumbai chuno. Par har US write ab bhi 190 ms deta hai, aur kai writes wala checkout ise mehsoos karta hai. Jab yeh manzoor na ho, ya residency rules lagu hon, toh har user ko ek home region do: shard key region se shuru hoti hai, aur har shard ka leader wahin baithta hai jahan uske users hain.

SQL: MySQL / Postgres

  • You choose the leader region per shard. Home-region placement means the shard key includes the region.Har shard ka leader region aap chunte ho. Home-region placement ka matlab shard key mein region shamil hai.
  • Distributed SQL databases (CockroachDB, Spanner, YugabyteDB) can pin rows to a region for you.Distributed SQL databases (CockroachDB, Spanner, YugabyteDB) rows ko aapke liye kisi region se jod sakte hain.

NoSQL: Cassandra / DynamoDB

  • There is no master to pick: every region accepts writes.Chunne ke liye koi master nahi: har region writes leta hai.
  • The question becomes "whose write wins in a conflict", so you still route each user's writes to one region to avoid conflicts.Sawaal ban jaata hai "conflict mein kiska write jeetega", isliye har user ke writes phir bhi ek hi region pe bhejo taaki conflicts na hon.

Active-passive vs active-active

Do regions mein database: ek chale ya dono

Problem

People say "just run both regions active-active" as if it were free. What does the second region actually do in each setup, what does it cost, and when is plain active-passive the better answer?

Log kehte hain "bas dono regions active-active chalao" jaise yeh free ho. Har setup mein doosra region asal mein kya karta hai, keemat kya hai, aur kab seedha active-passive hi behtar jawab hai?

Solution

Active-passive: one region writes, one waits

All writes go to Mumbai. Virginia receives async replication and may serve read-only traffic. On a disaster you promote Virginia. Simple, no conflicts, strong consistency on the leader; but US writes pay 190 ms, Virginia's capacity mostly waits, and failover takes minutes with RPO equal to the lag.

Saare writes Mumbai jaate hain. Virginia ko async replication milta hai aur woh read-only traffic serve kar sakta hai. Disaster pe Virginia ko promote karte ho. Active-active by partition: both write, each row has one home

Both regions take writes, but every row has exactly one leader, in its user's home region. Each region is the standby for the other's shards. Everyone gets local writes and there are no conflicts on a user's own data. The cost is routing logic, cross-region operations (a US buyer and an Indian seller in one order), and travellers.

Dono regions writes lete hain, par har row ka bilkul ek leader hota hai, uske user ke home region mein. Har region doosre ke shards ka standby hai. Sabko local writes milte hain aur user ke apne data pe koi conflict nahi. Keemat: routing logic, cross-region operations (ek order mein US buyer aur Indian seller), aur safar karne wale users.

Both regions accept writes to the same data and replicate both ways. Local writes and full availability even when the link breaks, but you must resolve write conflicts, and invariants such as "stock never below zero" or "usernames are unique" cannot be enforced across regions.

Problem

Put it together for ShopKart: 50M users split 70/30, 64 shards, local reads, writes in each user's home region, survival of a whole-region disaster, and no split-brain.

Shard key and strategy

Data ko kis key se aur kaise baantein

Problem

Users, orders and carts must be spread over 64 shards. Split by the wrong key and one shard melts while the rest idle, or every page has to ask all 64 shards. How do we choose the key and the method?

Users, orders aur carts ko 64 shards mein phailana hai. Galat key se baanto toh ek shard pighalta hai jabki baaki khaali baithe hain, ya har page ko saare 64 shards se poochhna padta hai. Key aur tareeka kaise chunein?

Solution

A good shard key

  1. Has many values (millions of users, not 30 cities).Uski bahut saari values hon (millions users, 30 shehar nahi).
  2. Spreads writes evenly: never "today's date", which sends all new writes to one shard.Writes ko barabar phailaye: "aaj ki date" kabhi nahi, jo saare naye writes ek shard pe bhejti hai.
  3. Appears in the common queries, so most requests touch one shard.Common queries mein ho, taaki zyaadatar requests ek hi shard chhuein.
  4. Keeps together what changes together: a user, their cart and their orders on one shard means one local transaction.Jo saath badalta hai use saath rakhe: user, uska cart aur uske orders ek shard pe, yaani ek local transaction.
  5. Never changes, because moving a row between shards is expensive.Kabhi na badle, kyunki row ko ek shard se doosre pe le jaana mehnga hai.

For ShopKart the key is userId, prefixed by the home region. Order IDs embed the bucket number, so an order lookup finds its shard without a directory.

ShopKart ke liye key hai userId, home region ke prefix ke saath. Order IDs mein bucket number chhupa hota hai, isliye order lookup bina directory ke apna shard dhoondh leta hai.

Resharding without downtime

Chalte system mein shard ko todna ya hilaana

Problem

Shard 7 holds 950 GB and its leader runs at 80% CPU. Half its buckets must move to a new shard while users keep reading and writing them, without losing a single write and without a maintenance window.

Shard 7 mein 950 GB hai aur uska leader 80% CPU pe chal raha hai. Iske aadhe buckets naye shard pe le jaane hain jabki users unme padhte-likhte rahein, ek bhi write khoye bina aur bina maintenance window ke.

  1. StartCreate shard 65 with its standby and remote replica. Nothing routes to it yet.Shard 65 banao, standby aur remote replica ke saath. Abhi koi traffic isme nahi jaata.

  3. ~1 minCatch up by replaying every change after P from the binlog, WAL or CDC stream. Each pass is shorter, because replay is faster than new writes.Catch up: P ke baad ke saare changes binlog, WAL ya CDC stream se replay karo. Har pass chhota hota hai, kyunki replay naye writes se tez hai.
  4. ~2 minVerify row counts and checksums of both copies at the same log position.Verify: dono copies ke row count aur checksum same log position pe milao.
  5. < 1 sCut over: reject writes for the moving buckets only, replay the last few changes, publish shard map v+1. Callers holding the old map get "moved" and refresh.Cut over: sirf hilne wale buckets ke writes roko, aakhri kuch changes replay karo, shard map v+1 publish karo. Replication lag and read-your-writes

Maine abhi likha, phir bhi purana kyun dikh raha hai?

Problem

In the active-passive setup a US user changes her delivery address. The write goes to the Mumbai leader, the page reloads, and the read hits the Virginia replica, which is 400 ms behind. The old address appears, she changes it again, and support later finds two orders shipped to two addresses.

  • Consistent prefix broken: you see an effect before its cause, such as "payment received" before the order exists.Consistent prefix toota: kaaran se pehle nateeja dikhta hai, jaise order bane bina "payment received".

CAP theorem, animated

Link toote toh: consistency chuno ya availability

Problem

The three letters

  • C, consistency: every read sees the latest write, as if there were only one copy.C, consistency: har read latest write dekhta hai, jaise sirf ek hi copy ho.

  • P, partition tolerance: the system keeps running when messages between regions are lost.P, partition tolerance: regions ke beech messages kho jaayein tab bhi system chalta rehta hai.

Management asks: "If Mumbai goes down, how much data do we lose and how long are we down?" "Hopefully not much" is not an answer. We need numbers, and a design that meets them.

Failover and split-brain

Mumbai mara hai ya sirf cable kata hai?

Problem

Virginia stops hearing from Mumbai. Is Mumbai dead, or is only the undersea link broken? If Virginia promotes itself while Mumbai is still alive and taking writes, both regions accept writes for the same shards: split-brain, and data that can never be merged cleanly.

Virginia ko Mumbai ki awaaz aana band ho gayi. Mumbai mar gaya hai, ya sirf samundar ke neeche ka link toota hai? Agar Virginia khud ko promote kar le jabki Mumbai zinda hai aur writes le raha hai, toh dono regions same shards pe writes lete hain: split-brain, aur aisa data jo kabhi saaf merge nahi hota.

Solution

A safe failover, second by second

  1. t = 0 sMumbai goes dark.Mumbai band ho jaata hai.
  2. t = 30 sVirginia's monitor misses 3 heartbeats, 10 s apart, and suspects Mumbai.Virginia ka monitor 10 s ke gap pe 3 heartbeats miss karta hai aur Mumbai pe shak karta hai.
  3. t = 31 sIt asks the witness in Frankfurt, which cannot reach Mumbai either: 2 of 3 votes, failover approved.Woh Frankfurt ke witness se poochhta hai, jo bhi Mumbai tak nahi pahunch pa raha: 3 mein se 2 votes, failover manzoor.
  4. t = 32 sFence: the epoch goes from 7 to 8, and storage, proxies and the shard map reject anything still signed with epoch 7.Fence: epoch 7 se 8 hota hai, aur storage, proxies aur shard map epoch 7 wali har cheez reject karte hain.
  5. t = 45 sFor each Mumbai-led shard, promote the Virginia replica with the highest applied log position.Mumbai ke har shard ke liye, sabse zyada applied log position wale Virginia replica ko promote karo.
  6. t = 50 sPublish shard map v+1: those shards now lead in Virginia.Shard map v+1 publish karo: ab woh shards Virginia mein lead karte hain.
  7. t = 111 sDNS TTL expires, Indian users reach Virginia, and writes work again at +190 ms.DNS TTL khatam, Indian users Virginia pahunchte hain, aur writes phir chalte hain, +190 ms ke saath.

Why two regions cannot decide alone

From Virginia, "Mumbai is dead" and "the cable is cut" look exactly the same: silence. With two voters there is no majority, so a third site, the witness, stores no data and only votes. If the cable is cut, the witness still hears Mumbai, votes no, and Virginia stays a read-only replica. US writes to Mumbai-led shards fail until the link heals: consistency is chosen over availability.

Virginia se "Mumbai mar gaya" aur "cable kat gaya" bilkul ek jaise dikhte hain: khamoshi. Do voters mein majority nahi banti, isliye teesri jagah, witness, koi data nahi rakhta aur sirf vote deta hai. Cable kata ho toh witness ko Mumbai ab bhi sunaai deta hai, woh na kehta hai, aur Virginia read-only replica bana rehta hai. Link theek hone tak Mumbai-led shards pe US writes fail hote hain: availability ki jagah consistency chuni gayi.

  • Fencing tokens: every leader holds an epoch number, and storage rejects writes with an older epoch, so a zombie leader that wakes up cannot corrupt data.Fencing tokens: har leader ke paas ek epoch number hai, aur storage purane epoch wale writes reject karta hai, taaki jaaga hua zombie leader data kharab na kare.
  • Failback: when Mumbai returns it rejoins as a replica, rewinds and saves its unshipped writes for reconciliation, catches up, and only then do you switch back in a planned way: stop writes, wait for zero lag, swap.Failback: Mumbai wapas aaye toh replica ki tarah judta hai, rewind karke apne na bheje writes reconcile ke liye bachata hai, catch up karta hai, aur tabhi planned tareeke se wapas switch karte ho: writes roko, lag zero hone do, swap karo.

SQL: MySQL / Postgres

  • Postgres: Patroni holds the leader key in etcd or Consul spread over three sites, so only a majority can elect a leader.Postgres: Patroni leader key etcd ya Consul mein rakhta hai jo teen jagah phaila hai, isliye sirf majority hi leader chun sakti hai.
  • MySQL: Orchestrator or InnoDB Cluster. Managed services such as Aurora Global Database or Cloud SQL promote a secondary region on request.MySQL: Orchestrator ya InnoDB Cluster. Aurora Global Database ya Cloud SQL jaisi managed services kehne pe secondary region promote karti hain.

NoSQL: Cassandra / DynamoDB

  • No promotion step: Virginia already takes writes, and clients with a data-centre-aware policy switch over.Koi promotion step nahi: Virginia pehle se writes leta hai, aur data-centre-aware policy wale clients switch kar lete hain.
  • A cable cut leaves both sides writable with LOCAL_QUORUM; when it heals, last writer wins decides. That is availability chosen over consistency.Cable kate toh LOCAL_QUORUM ke saath dono taraf writes chalte hain; theek hone pe last writer wins faisla karta hai. Yeh consistency ki jagah availability chunna hai.

Disaster recovery and HA upkeep

Disaster recovery aur high availability ko roz zinda rakhna

Problem

The design doc says "two regions, standby ready, RPO 1 s, RTO 2 min". Six months later the standby is missing two schema changes, the failover script points at a server that was replaced, nobody has restored a backup since launch, and the on-call engineer has never run a failover. High availability decays unless you maintain it and prove it.

Design doc kehta hai "do regions, standby taiyaar, RPO 1 s, RTO 2 min". Chhe mahine baad standby mein do schema changes missing hain, failover script ek badle gaye server ki taraf point karti hai, launch ke baad kisi ne backup restore nahi kiya, aur on-call engineer ne kabhi failover chalaya hi nahi. Solution

HA and DR are two different jobs

High availability (HA)

  • Survives small, frequent failures: a node, a disk, a zone.Chhote, aksar hone wale failures jhelta hai: node, disk, zone.

  • Survives rare, big events: a whole region, data corruption, ransomware, a bad deploy.Kabhi-kabhar ke bade events jhelta hai: poora region, data corruption, ransomware, kharab deploy.
  • Planned RPO and RTO, often with a human decision; backups matter as much as replicas.Tay RPO aur RTO, aksar insaan ke faisle ke saath; backups utne hi zaroori jitne replicas.

Users are 70% in India and 30% in the US. Where do you put the master database, and why?

70% users India mein aur 30% US mein hain. Master database kahan rakhoge, aur kyun?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Start with the law: if Indian users' data must stay in India, their leader is in Mumbai. Then follow the writes: with one master, Mumbai costs 0.7 × 2 + 0.3 × 190 = 58.4 ms per write on average against 133.6 ms for Virginia, so Mumbai wins. But US users still pay 190 ms on every write, so for a real product I'd give each user a home region: the shard key includes the region, Indian users' shards lead in Mumbai, US users' shards lead in Virginia, and each region keeps an async replica of the other's shards for disaster recovery.

Q2Easy

Sync vs async replication across regions: what does each cost?

Regions ke beech sync vs async replication: dono ki keemat kya hai?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Async acknowledges after the local commit, about 2 ms, but the other region lags, so a region disaster loses the unshipped writes: about one second, or roughly 6,945 writes at peak. Sync waits for the far replica: 2 + 190 = 192 ms per commit and, by Little's Law, 1,333 commits in flight at peak, and writes stall if the far side is slow or unreachable. The usual answer is sync across zones inside a region and async across regions, with sync kept for small, critical writes such as payments.

Async local commit ke baad OK deta hai, lagbhag 2 ms, par doosra region peeche rehta hai, isliye region disaster mein na bheje writes kho jaate hain: lagbhag ek second, yaani peak pe ~6,945 writes. Sync door wale replica ka intezaar karta hai: har commit 2 + 190 = 192 ms, Little's Law se peak pe 1,333 commits in flight, aur door wala slow ya unreachable ho toh writes ruk jaate hain. Aam jawab: region ke andar zones ke beech sync, regions ke beech async, aur sync sirf chhote, critical writes jaise payments ke liye.

Q3Medium

How many shards do you need for 50 TB and about 7,000 writes per second? Walk through the math.

50 TB aur lagbhag 7,000 writes per second ke liye kitne shards chahiye? Hisaab samjhao.

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Size by each limit and take the tighter one. Storage: 6 TB today plus 40 GB a day for 3 years is 49.8 TB; at 1 TB per shard that is 50 shards, and 1 TB keeps a rebuild near an hour. Writes: 6,944 ÷ (4,000 × 0.6) = 3 shards. The max is 50, storage-bound. I round up to 64 so each future split is a clean halving, with 1,024 logical buckets, 16 per shard. Three copies per shard gives 192 database nodes. Reads, about 1,085/s per shard, need no extra replicas.

Har limit se size karo aur tight wali lo. Storage: aaj 6 TB plus 3 saal tak 40 GB roz = 49.8 TB; 1 TB per shard pe 50 shards, aur 1 TB rebuild ko lagbhag ek ghante mein rakhta hai. Writes: 6,944 ÷ (4,000 × 0.6) = 3 shards. Max 50, storage-bound. Main 64 tak round up karta hoon taaki har future split saaf aadha ho, 1,024 logical buckets ke saath, har shard mein 16. Har shard ki teen copies se 192 database nodes. Reads, lagbhag 1,085/s per shard, ko extra replicas nahi chahiye.

Q4Medium

Active-passive vs active-active: when would you choose each?

Active-passive vs active-active: kab kaunsa chunoge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Active-passive puts all writes in one region; the other gets async replication and maybe read traffic. It is simple with no conflicts, but remote users pay the round trip, the standby mostly idles, and failover takes minutes with RPO equal to the lag. Active-active by partition gives each row one home region, so both regions write locally without conflicts, at the cost of routing and cross-region workflows. Multi-leader active-active lets any region write any row: always available, but it needs conflict resolution and cannot enforce invariants. I start active-passive, move to partitioned when both regions write heavily or residency applies, and keep multi-leader for mergeable data only.

Active-passive saare writes ek region mein rakhta hai; doosre ko async replication aur shayad read traffic milta hai. Simple hai, koi conflict nahi, par door ke users round trip dete hain, standby zyaadatar khaali, aur failover minutes leta hai jisme RPO lag jitna. Partition wala active-active har row ko ek home region deta hai, toh dono regions bina conflict local likhte hain, routing aur cross-region workflows ki keemat pe. Multi-leader active-active mein koi bhi region koi bhi row likhta hai: hamesha available, par conflict resolution chahiye aur niyam laagu nahi hote. Main active-passive se shuru karta hoon, dono regions bhaari likhein ya residency ho toh partition wale pe jaata hoon, aur multi-leader sirf merge hone wale data ke liye.

Q5Hard

The Mumbai region goes down. Walk through the failover, with RPO and RTO numbers.

Mumbai region down ho gaya. Failover step by step batao, RPO aur RTO numbers ke saath.

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Virginia's monitor misses 3 heartbeats 10 s apart (30 s) and asks the Frankfurt witness, which cannot see Mumbai either: 2 of 3 votes. It fences the old leaders by bumping the epoch, promotes the most caught-up Virginia replica of each Mumbai-led shard (highest applied LSN), and publishes a new shard map version. After the 60 s DNS TTL, Indian users reach Virginia. RTO ≈ 30 + 1 + 20 + 60 ≈ 2 minutes. RPO is the async lag at failure, about 1 s or roughly 4,900 writes on Mumbai-led shards; those writes stay on Mumbai's disk and are reconciled when it returns as a replica, before a planned failback.

Virginia ka monitor 10 s ke gap pe 3 heartbeats miss karta hai (30 s) aur Frankfurt ke witness se poochhta hai, jise bhi Mumbai nahi dikhta: 3 mein se 2 votes. Epoch badha kar purane leaders ko fence karta hai, har Mumbai-led shard ke sabse zyada caught-up Virginia replica (sabse bada applied LSN) ko promote karta hai, aur naya shard map version publish karta hai. 60 s DNS TTL ke baad Indian users Virginia pahunchte hain. RTO ≈ 30 + 1 + 20 + 60 ≈ 2 minute. RPO failure ke waqt ka async lag hai, lagbhag 1 s yaani Mumbai-led shards pe ~4,900 writes; woh writes Mumbai ki disk pe rehte hain aur jab woh replica ban kar lautta hai tab reconcile hote hain, planned failback se pehle.

Q6Hard

How do you prevent split-brain with only two regions?

Sirf do regions ke saath split-brain kaise rokoge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

From Virginia, a dead Mumbai and a cut cable look identical, and two voters cannot form a majority, so either side deciding alone risks two leaders. Add a third site: a witness that stores no data and only votes (Frankfurt is about 6,560 km from both). Failover needs 2 of 3 votes, and after a cable cut the witness still hears Mumbai and votes no. Add fencing: every leader carries an epoch and storage rejects older epochs, so a returning zombie leader cannot write. Without a witness, make region failover a human decision.

Virginia se mara hua Mumbai aur kata cable ek jaise dikhte hain, aur do voters majority nahi bana sakte, isliye koi bhi side akele faisla kare toh do leaders ka khatra hai. Teesri jagah jodo: ek witness jo data nahi rakhta, sirf vote deta hai (Frankfurt dono se lagbhag 6,560 km). Failover ko 3 mein se 2 votes chahiye, aur cable kata ho toh witness ko Mumbai sunaai deta hai aur woh na kehta hai. Fencing jodo: har leader ek epoch rakhta hai aur storage purane epoch reject karta hai, taaki laut-ta zombie leader likh na sake. Witness na ho toh region failover insaan ka faisla rakho.

Q7Medium

A user updates their address and still sees the old one. Why, and how do you fix it?

User address badalta hai aur use purana hi dikhta hai. Kyun, aur kaise theek karoge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Replication lag: the write went to the leader, and the next read hit a replica that had not replayed it yet. Fix read-your-writes: return the commit's log position (LSN or GTID), keep it in the session, and let a replica serve that user's read only once it has applied at least that position; otherwise wait briefly or read the leader. A simpler option is to read from the leader for a few seconds after the user writes. Sticky replicas fix monotonic reads.

Replication lag: write leader pe gaya, aur agla read aise replica pe gaya jisne use abhi replay nahi kiya tha. Read-your-writes theek karo: commit ki log position (LSN ya GTID) lautao, session mein rakho, aur replica us user ka read tabhi serve kare jab usne kam se kam woh position apply kar li ho; warna thoda ruko ya leader se padho. Aasaan option: user ke likhne ke baad kuch seconds leader se padho. Sticky replicas monotonic reads theek karte hain.

Q8Medium

How do you choose a shard key? Give a bad and a good example.

Shard key kaise chunoge? Ek buri aur ek achhi misaal do.

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

It needs many values, an even write spread, presence in the common queries, co-location of data that changes together, and it must never change. Bad: order date, where every new write hits one shard, or country, with only a few dozen values and India far bigger than the rest. Good for ShopKart: userId prefixed with the home region and hashed into 1,024 buckets. A user's cart and orders live together, and order IDs embed the bucket so lookups need no directory.

Uski bahut values hon, writes barabar phailein, common queries mein ho, saath badalne wala data saath rahe, aur woh kabhi na badle. Buri: order date, jahan har naya write ek shard pe jaata hai, ya country, jiski kuch darjan values hain aur India baakiyon se bahut bada. ShopKart ke liye achhi: home region prefix wala userId, 1,024 buckets mein hash. User ka cart aur orders saath rehte hain, aur order IDs mein bucket hota hai taaki lookup ko directory na chahiye.

Q9Hard

How do you reshard a live 1 TB shard without downtime?

Chalte hue 1 TB shard ko bina downtime reshard kaise karoge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Move logical buckets, never rehash keys. Create the target shard, record the source log position P, and bulk-copy a consistent snapshot of the moving buckets at P (500 GB at 200 MB/s ≈ 42 min, no locks). Replay changes after P from the binlog or WAL until the backlog is tiny; it converges because replay outruns new writes. Verify checksums at one position. Cut over: reject writes for the moving buckets only, for under a second, replay the last changes and publish shard map v+1; stale routers get a "moved" error and refresh. Keep the old copy for rollback. Vitess and Citus automate this.

Logical buckets hilao, keys kabhi rehash nahi. Target shard banao, source ki log position P note karo, aur P pe moving buckets ka consistent snapshot bulk-copy karo (500 GB 200 MB/s pe ≈ 42 min, bina locks). P ke baad ke changes binlog ya WAL se replay karo jab tak backlog chhota na ho; yeh converge hota hai kyunki replay naye writes se tez hai. Ek position pe checksums verify karo. Cut over: sirf moving buckets ke writes ek second se kam roko, aakhri changes replay karo aur shard map v+1 publish karo; purane routers ko "moved" error milta hai aur woh refresh karte hain. Rollback ke liye purani copy rakho. Vitess aur Citus yeh automate karte hain.

Q10Medium

One product gets 5,000 writes per second in a flash sale. What do you do?

Flash sale mein ek product pe 5,000 writes per second aate hain. Kya karoge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

It is a hot key, so more shards will not help: one row lives on one shard, and a leader takes 2,400 writes/s at target. Split the key: ⌈5,000 ÷ 2,400⌉ = 3, rounded to 4 pieces on different shards, about 1,250 writes/s each. For stock, split the units into 4 piles and decrement a random pile; it is sold out when all are empty. Reads combine the pieces. Also cache hot reads, batch counters, rate-limit, and detect hot keys early with per-key counters. In DynamoDB this is write sharding with a suffix.

Yeh hot key hai, isliye zyada shards se kuch nahi hoga: ek row ek shard pe rehti hai, aur leader target pe 2,400 writes/s leta hai. Key ko todo: ⌈5,000 ÷ 2,400⌉ = 3, round karke 4 pieces alag shards pe, har ek lagbhag 1,250 writes/s. Stock ke liye units ko 4 dheriyon mein baanto aur random dheri ghatao; sab khaali hon tab sold out. Reads pieces jodte hain. Saath mein hot reads cache karo, counters batch karo, rate-limit lagao, aur per-key counters se hot keys pehle pakdo. DynamoDB mein yeh suffix wali write sharding hai.

Q11Hard

Two regions write the same cart at the same time. How are conflicts resolved, and what can go wrong?

Do regions ek hi cart ek saath likhte hain. Conflicts kaise suljhenge, aur kya galat ho sakta hai?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

First avoid it by routing each cart's writes to its home region. If multi-leader is unavoidable, detect concurrency with version vectors. Last writer wins silently drops one update, and clock skew can make the older write win. A union merge keeps both items but lets deleted items reappear; an OR-Set CRDT merges adds and removes correctly. Cassandra and DynamoDB global tables use last writer wins, so design the cart as mergeable data or keep one writer per cart. Never run money or stock multi-leader.

Pehle bacho: har cart ke writes uske home region pe bhejo. Multi-leader zaroori ho toh version vectors se concurrency pakdo. Last writer wins chupchaap ek update gira deta hai, aur clock skew purane write ko jita sakta hai. Union merge dono items rakhta hai par delete hue items wapas aa sakte hain; OR-Set CRDT add aur remove dono sahi merge karta hai. Cassandra aur DynamoDB global tables last writer wins use karte hain, isliye cart ko merge hone wale data ki tarah design karo ya har cart ka ek hi writer rakho. Paisa ya stock kabhi multi-leader mat chalao.

Q12Medium

Explain R + W > N with Cassandra across two data centres.

Do data centres wale Cassandra mein R + W > N samjhao.

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

N is the replica count, W the acknowledgements a write waits for, R the replicas a read asks. If R + W > N, every read set overlaps the latest write set, so reads see the latest acknowledged write. With 3 replicas per data centre, LOCAL_QUORUM (2 + 2 > 3) is strong within a region at local latency and eventual across regions. EACH_QUORUM writes with LOCAL_QUORUM reads are strong everywhere, but writes pay +190 ms. Global QUORUM needs 4 of 6, which always crosses the ocean. Quorums do not give compare-and-set; use lightweight transactions for that.

N replicas ki ginti, W write kitne acknowledgements ka intezaar kare, R read kitne replicas se poochhe. R + W > N ho toh har read set latest write set se milta hai, isliye reads latest acknowledged write dekhte hain. Har data centre mein 3 replicas ke saath, LOCAL_QUORUM (2 + 2 > 3) region ke andar local latency pe strong aur regions ke beech eventual. EACH_QUORUM writes aur LOCAL_QUORUM reads har jagah strong, par writes +190 ms dete hain. Global QUORUM ko 6 mein se 4 chahiye, jo hamesha ocean paar karta hai. Quorums compare-and-set nahi dete; uske liye lightweight transactions lo.

Q13Medium

How do you answer queries that don't include the shard key?

Jin queries mein shard key nahi hai, unka jawab kaise doge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Scatter-gather to all shards is simple but costs 64× the work, and tail latency grows: with a 20 ms p99 per shard, 47% of queries hit a slow shard. Better options are a global secondary index sharded by the other key and kept in sync by CDC, denormalized copies (an order stored under both buyer and seller), or a search or analytics store fed from Kafka. Keep the main paths on one shard; scatter only for rare admin queries, with per-shard timeouts, a partial-result flag, and local replicas so the fan-out never crosses regions.

Saare shards pe scatter-gather simple hai par 64 guna kaam leta hai, aur tail latency badhti hai: har shard ka p99 20 ms ho toh 47% queries slow shard pe atakti hain. Behtar options: doosri key se sharded global secondary index jo CDC se sync rahe, denormalized copies (order buyer aur seller dono ke under), ya Kafka se bhara search ya analytics store. Main raaste ek shard pe rakho; scatter sirf kabhi-kabhar ki admin queries ke liye, har shard ke timeout, partial-result flag aur local replicas ke saath taaki fan-out kabhi regions paar na kare.

Q14Hard

How do you do a money transfer across two shards in different regions?

Alag regions ke do shards ke beech money transfer kaise karoge?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Co-location avoids most cross-shard work, but a transfer between two users cannot be co-located. 2PC gives atomicity but costs at least 2 round trips (≥ 380 ms) with locks held, and it can block if the coordinator dies, so across regions I use a saga: debit the sender in one local transaction, credit the receiver in another, and if the credit fails for good, refund the sender as a compensation. Every step uses an idempotency key, a durable saga log lets a restart resume, the outbox pattern publishes events reliably, amounts are integer paise, and the UI shows "transfer pending" in between.

Co-location zyaadatar cross-shard kaam se bacha leta hai, par do users ke beech transfer co-locate nahi ho sakta. 2PC atomicity deta hai par kam se kam 2 round trips (≥ 380 ms) locks ke saath leta hai, aur coordinator mare toh block kar sakta hai, isliye regions ke paar main saga use karta hoon: sender ko ek local transaction mein debit, receiver ko doosre mein credit, aur credit hamesha ke liye fail ho toh compensation ki tarah sender ko refund. Har step idempotency key use karta hai, durable saga log se restart resume hota hai, outbox pattern events bharose se publish karta hai, amounts poore paise mein, aur beech mein UI "transfer pending" dikhata hai.

Q15Easy

Is replication a backup? What else do you need?

Kya replication backup hai? Aur kya chahiye?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

No. Replication copies mistakes too: a bad DELETE or a buggy migration reaches every replica in milliseconds. You also need point-in-time backups (snapshots plus log archives) copied to another region, restores that you test regularly (1 TB takes about 67 minutes at 250 MB/s), and ideally a delayed replica for fast recovery from human error.

Nahi. Replication galtiyan bhi copy karta hai: galat DELETE ya buggy migration milliseconds mein har replica tak pahunch jaata hai. Aapko point-in-time backups (snapshots plus log archives) bhi chahiye jo doosre region mein copy hon, aise restores jinhe regularly test karo (250 MB/s pe 1 TB lagbhag 67 minute), aur insaani galti se jaldi ubharne ke liye ek delayed replica.

Q16Medium

SQL or NoSQL for ShopKart's multi-region order data? Defend your choice.

ShopKart ke multi-region order data ke liye SQL ya NoSQL? Apni choice defend karo.

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Orders need transactions (order, items and payment status together), strong reads for the owner and ad-hoc queries, so I would pick sharded SQL, MySQL with Vitess or Postgres with Citus, with a home region per user and async cross-region replicas. Q17Medium

Explain the CAP theorem using this two-region design. Which parts are CP and which are AP?

CAP theorem ko is two-region design se samjhao. Kaunse hisse CP hain aur kaunse AP?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

CAP says that while the link between regions is broken, a system must choose between consistency (every read sees the latest write) and availability (every live region answers). Partitions will happen across an ocean, so the choice is real. ShopKart makes it per data type. Stock, payments and usernames are CP: a region cut off from the majority (the other region plus the Frankfurt witness) rejects those writes, so nothing is oversold or charged twice. Carts, likes, sessions and feeds are AP: both regions keep accepting them and merge later with CRDTs or last-writer-wins. PACELC adds that even without a partition we trade latency for consistency: synchronous cross-region writes cost 190 ms.

CAP kehta hai ki jab regions ke beech link toota ho, system ko consistency (har read latest write dekhe) aur availability (har zinda region jawab de) mein se chunna padta hai. Ocean ke paar partitions honge hi, isliye yeh choice asli hai. ShopKart yeh har data type ke liye karta hai. Stock, payments aur usernames CP hain: majority (doosra region aur Frankfurt witness) se kata hua region in writes ko mana karta hai, taaki kuch oversell ya do baar charge na ho. Carts, likes, sessions aur feeds AP hain: dono regions inhe lete rehte hain aur baad mein CRDTs ya last-writer-wins se merge karte hain. PACELC jodta hai ki partition na ho tab bhi hum latency aur consistency ka trade karte hain: synchronous cross-region writes 190 ms lete hain.

Q18Hard

How do you keep a two-region database highly available month after month, and how do you prove disaster recovery actually works?

Do-region database ko mahine dar mahine highly available kaise rakhoge, aur kaise prove karoge ki disaster recovery sach mein kaam karti hai?

Answer out loud first, then reveal.

Separate HA from DR. HA is automatic inside a region: a synchronous standby in another zone, an orchestrator that promotes in about 30 s, and N+1 app servers. DR covers losing a region or corrupting data: an async replica in the other region with a witness, plus point-in-time backups. Then maintain it: rolling upgrades with planned switchovers (wait for lag 0, swap, zero data loss), online schema changes with expand–contract, a standby kept identical in schema, config and size, and alerts on lag trend, backup age and standby drift. Finally prove it: restore a random shard's backup every week, run a real regional switchover every quarter and measure RPO and RTO, run chaos tests, and spend the error budget (4.4 minutes a month at 99.99%) on these drills.

HA aur DR ko alag rakho. HA region ke andar automatic hai: doosre zone mein synchronous standby, ek orchestrator jo lagbhag 30 s mein promote kare, aur N+1 app servers. DR region khone ya data kharab hone ke liye hai: witness ke saath doosre region mein async replica, aur point-in-time backups. Phir ise maintain karo: planned switchovers ke saath rolling upgrades (lag 0 hone tak ruko, swap karo, zero data loss), expand–contract se online schema changes, schema, config aur size mein bilkul same standby, aur lag trend, backup age aur standby drift pe alerts. Aakhir mein prove karo: har hafte kisi random shard ka backup restore karo, har quarter asli regional switchover chalao aur RPO aur RTO naapo, chaos tests chalao, aur error budget (99.99% pe mahine ke 4.4 minute) inhi drills pe kharch karo.
