Requirements gathering — problem ko pin down karo

SDE-1SDE-2SDE-3

Interviewer bolega

“Design a parking lot system.”

Bas itna. Ismein kuch bhi define nahi hai — na size, na vehicle types, na payment. Ye deliberately vague hota hai. Interviewer dekh raha hai ki tum seedha code likhne lagte ho ya pehle problem ko boundaries dete ho.

Jo cheezein missing hain

  • Kitni floors? Kitne spots? Ek lot ya multiple lots?
  • Kaunse vehicles — bike, car, truck, EV?
  • Payment kaise — cash, card, UPI, monthly pass?
  • Entry/exit gates kitne? Automated barrier hai ya manual?
  • Reservation/booking chahiye ya walk-in only?
MOST COMMON MISTAKE 90% candidates yahin fail karte hain — 30 second mein hi class Car likhna shuru kar dete hain. Pehle 5–7 minutes requirements pe kharch karo. Ye time waste nahi hai, ye interview ka scored part hai.

Tumhara jawaab

Requirements ko teen buckets me todo, aur har bucket ko explicitly bolo. Main ek single physical parking lot assume kar raha hoon, aur SDE-3 section me isko multi-lot distributed system tak scale karunga.

Functional requirements (in scope)

  • Multi-floor lot; har floor pe multiple spot types (small / medium / large / EV / handicapped).
  • Vehicle entry pe ticket issue ho, spot automatically assign ho.
  • Exit pe fee calculate ho (duration + spot type + rate card ke basis pe).
  • Multiple payment modes: cash, card, UPI.
  • Real-time availability display — per floor, per spot type.
  • Pluggable spot-allocation strategy (nearest gate / lowest floor / max free floor).

Explicitly out of scope (bolna zaroori hai)

  • Valet parking, car wash, EV charging billing.
  • Number-plate OCR / ANPR ka ML model.
  • Monthly subscription passes (mention karo, phir park kar do).

  • 1 lot = 5 floors × 200 spots = 1000 spots.
  • Peak turnover: har spot din me ~4 baar → 4000 entries/din.

A‑02

Clarifying questions — exactly kya poochhna hai

SDE-1SDE-2

Problem

Questions poochhna hai — lekin kaunse? Bahut candidates ya to kuch nahi poochhte, ya 20 random questions daag dete hain jinse design change hi nahi hota.

Rule

Sirf wo question poochho jiska answer tumhara design badal de. Agar dono jawaabon pe same code likhoge, to wo question bekaar hai.

BEKAAR QUESTION “Lot ka naam kya hai?” — iska answer design nahi badalta.
ACHCHA QUESTION “Ek truck do chhote spots le sakta hai kya?” — agar haan, to spot allocation ka poora algorithm badal jaayega (contiguous multi-spot allocation).

10 questions jo actually design badalte hain

  1. Ek vehicle multiple spots le sakti hai? Agar haan → contiguous-block allocation chahiye, simple lookup se kaam nahi chalega.
  2. Spot types fixed hain ya dynamic reconfigurable? Dynamic → spot type ko DB-driven rakhna padega, enum hard-code nahi.
  3. Pricing kaise — flat hourly ya slab-based? Slab → Strategy pattern zaroori, warna ek method bhi kaafi hai.
  4. Kitne entry/exit gates? Multiple → concurrent allocation ka race condition central problem ban jaata hai.
  5. Ticket kho jaaye to? → Lost-ticket flow + max-fee policy + vehicle number se recovery lookup chahiye.
  6. Advance booking / reservation chahiye? Haan → spot ka RESERVED state + expiry timer + hold-release job.
  7. Payment gate pe hoga ya kiosk pe (pre-payment)? Kiosk → ticket me PAID state aur grace period (~15 min) add karna hoga.
  8. Offline support chahiye — network down ho to gate kaam kare? Haan → local write-ahead log + later sync (SDE-3 ka favourite follow-up).
  9. Ek system ya multiple lots (chain like mall parking)? Multiple → lot_id se sharding, central availability service.
  10. Handicapped/EV spots ka priority rule kya hai? → Allocation strategy me eligibility filter add hoga.
SCRIPT JO BOL SAKTE HO “Main pehle scope lock kar leta hoon. Main assume kar raha hoon: single lot, one vehicle = one spot, exit pe payment, aur multiple gates. In assumptions me koi change karna chahenge?” — assumption bolo, phir confirm karo. Isse silence bhi nahi hoti aur control tumhare paas rehta hai.
A‑03

HLD — high level architecture

SDE-2SDE-3

Problem

Gaadi gate pe aayi. Us moment se lekar exit tak, kaunse components involve hain aur data kahan flow karta hai? Interviewer boxes-and-arrows chahta hai, saath me ye justification ki har box alag kyun hai.

Design tension

Do cheezein aapas me clash karti hain:

  • Allocation ko strongly consistent hona chahiye (double-booking na ho).
  • Availability display ko fast aur cheap hona chahiye (har second 100 log dekh rahe hain).

Dono ko ek hi path pe daaloge to ya to display slow hoga, ya allocation pe load aayega.

Kya justify karna hai

  • Gate ek thin client kyun hai, smart device kyun nahi?
  • Availability alag service kyun?
  • SQL kyun, NoSQL kyun nahi?

Architecture

Availability Service DB ko touch hi nahi karti — wo Redis counters se padhti hai jo Kafka events se update hote hain. Isse display ka read load write path se poori tarah alag ho jaata hai.

Sequence diagram — entry aur exit flow

Problem

Class diagram structure dikhata hai. Lekin interviewer poochhega: “Ek gaadi aayi — step by step kya hota hai?” Iske liye behaviour chahiye, structure nahi.

Sequence diagram me kya dikhana zaroori hai

  • Failure branch — spot na mile to? Payment fail ho to?

Entry flow

Exit flow

Enums, Money aur Vehicle — foundation classes

SDE-1

Problem

Sabse pehle wo cheezein banao jinpe baaki sab depend karega: vehicle types, spot types, aur paisa.

Do decisions jo yahan lene hain

  1. “Kya ye gaadi is spot me fit hogi?” ka knowledge kahan rakhein?
    Option A: Vehicle.canFitIn(spot)    Option B: Spot.canFitVehicle(vehicle)
  2. Paisa kis type me rakhein? double, int paise, ya dedicated Money class?
DOUBLE SE PAISA — NEVER 0.1 + 0.2 ka result Java me 0.30000000000000004 hai. 10,000 transactions ke baad accounts mismatch ho jaayenge. Interviewer specifically is cheez ko notice karta hai. Hamesha BigDecimal ya minor units (long paise) use karo.

Fit-logic kahan?

Answer: Spot pe. Kyun? Kyunki capacity spot ka property hai. Ek Honda City ko nahi pata ki mall ka stall kitna bada hai. Naya spot type add karoge (jaise OVERSIZED) to sirf SpotType badlega, saari Vehicle subclasses nahi.

public enum SpotType {

    SMALL(1),
    MEDIUM(2),
    LARGE(4),
    ELECTRIC(2),
    HANDICAPPED(2);

C‑08

ParkingSpot — jahan race condition chhupi hoti hai

SDE-1SDE-2

Problem

Ek spot ka kaam simple lagta hai: free hai ya occupied. Zyadatar candidates ye likhte hain:

Naive version✗ broken
private boolean occupied;

public boolean isFree() {
    return !occupied;
}

public void park(Vehicle vehicle) {
    this.occupied = true;
    this.parkedVehicle = vehicle;
}

Caller aisa code likhega:

Caller✗ check-then-act
if (spot.isFree()) {      // Thread A: true
                          // Thread B: true (!)
    spot.park(vehicle);   // dono park kar gaye
}
YE FAIL KARTA HAI Do gates, ek hi last spot. Dono threads isFree() ko true dekhte hain, dono park() call karte hain. Result: ek stall pe do gaadiyaan allot — physical conflict, aur customer gaali dega. Ye check-then-act race condition hai: check aur act ke beech gap hai.

Fix ka principle

Check aur act ko ek atomic operation banao. Interface se isFree() hata do — agar caller check hi nahi kar sakta, to race likhna impossible ho jaata hai. Isko API design se bug rokna kehte hain.

Thread-safe ParkingSpot

ParkingSpot.java✓ atomic claim
public class ParkingSpot {

    private final String spotId;
    private final SpotType spotType;
    private final int floorNumber;
    private final int walkingDistanceFromGate;

    private final AtomicBoolean occupied = new AtomicBoolean(false);
    private volatile Vehicle parkedVehicle;

    public ParkingSpot(String spotId,
                       SpotType spotType,
                       int floorNumber,
                       int walkingDistanceFromGate) {
        this.spotId = spotId;
        this.spotType = spotType;
        this.floorNumber = floorNumber;
        this.walkingDistanceFromGate = walkingDistanceFromGate;
    }

    public boolean canFitVehicle(Vehicle vehicle) {
        return spotType.canAccommodate(vehicle.getVehicleType());
    }

    public boolean tryOccupy(Vehicle vehicle) {
        if (!canFitVehicle(vehicle)) {
            return false;
        }
        if (!occupied.compareAndSet(false, true)) {
            return false;
        }
        this.parkedVehicle = vehicle;
        return true;
    }

    public void release() {
        this.parkedVehicle = null;
        this.occupied.set(false);
    }

    public boolean isOccupied() {
        return occupied.get();
    }

    public String getSpotId() { return spotId; }
    public SpotType getSpotType() { return spotType; }
    public int getFloorNumber() { return floorNumber; }
    public int getWalkingDistanceFromGate() { return walkingDistanceFromGate; }
}
  • 3–6

    Saari identity fields final — spot ka location aur type kabhi nahi badalta. Sirf occupancy mutable hai. Mutable surface jitna chhota, utne kam bugs.

  • 6

    walkingDistanceFromGate — ye field allocation strategy ke liye hai (nearest spot dhoondhne ke liye). Naam se hi clear hai ki ye pedal distance hai, straight-line nahi.

  • 8

    Ye line hi asli answer hai. AtomicBoolean hardware ka CAS (compare-and-swap) instruction use karta hai. Iska matlab “check + set” CPU level pe ek hi indivisible operation hai — beech me koi dusra thread ghus hi nahi sakta.

  • 9

    volatile Vehicle — jab thread A vehicle set kare, thread B ko turant dikhe. volatile ke bina JVM value ko CPU cache me rakh sakta hai aur dusre core ko purani value dikhegi (visibility problem).

  • 21–23

    canFitVehicle — pure function, koi state nahi badalta. Naam question jaisa hai, isliye caller ko pata hai ki safe hai.

  • 25

    Method ka naam tryOccupy, park nahi. try prefix ka matlab: “fail ho sakta hai, return value check karo”. Ye Java ki standard convention hai (tryLock, tryAcquire). Naam se hi API ka contract clear ho jaata hai.

  • 26–28

    Size check pehle — sasta hai, aur agar fit nahi hota to CAS pe CPU waste karne ka koi matlab nahi.

  • 29

    compareAndSet(false, true): “agar abhi false hai to true kar do”. Return true hua matlab maine claim kiya. false hua matlab kisi aur ne pehle le liya. Do threads me se exactly ek hi true paayega — ye JVM ki guarantee hai.

  • 32

    Vehicle assign CAS ke baad hota hai. Is line tak pahunchne ka matlab hai ki maine spot jeet liya hai, ab koi race nahi bacha.

  • 36–39

    release() me order ulta hai: pehle vehicle hataya, phir flag false kiya. Kyunki flag false hote hi doosra thread claim kar sakta hai — us waqt tak purana vehicle reference saaf hona chahiye.

API DESIGN LESSON Public interface me isFree() hai hi nahi, sirf isOccupied() hai jo monitoring/display ke liye hai. Allocation ka ekmatra raasta tryOccupy() hai. Caller chahe bhi to race condition nahi likh sakta. Achhi API galat use karna mushkil bana deti hai.
SDE-3 FOLLOW-UP “Multiple JVM instances honge to AtomicBoolean kaam karega?” — Nahi. CAS sirf ek process ke andar valid hai. Distributed setup me DB ka conditional update chahiye: UPDATE spot SET status='OCCUPIED' WHERE id=? AND status='FREE' aur rowsAffected == 1 check karo. Poori detail section 14 me.
C‑09

ParkingFloor aur ParkingLot — O(1) allocation

SDE-1SDE-2

Problem

Floor ke paas 200 spots hain. Ek car aayi. Free spot kaise dhoondhein?

Naive scan✗ O(n)
for (ParkingSpot spot : allSpots) {
    if (!spot.isOccupied() && spot.canFitVehicle(v)) {
        return spot;
    }
}

Ye chalta to hai, lekin do problems hain:

  • O(n) har request pe. Lot 95% bhara ho to har baar 190 spots scan karne padenge sirf 10 free dhoondhne ke liye.
  • Race condition wapas aa gayi. isOccupied() check karke spot return kiya — caller ke claim karne se pehle koi aur le sakta hai.

Kya chahiye

  • Free spot nikaalna O(1) ho.
  • Nikaalna aur claim karna atomic ho.
  • Per spot-type alag pool ho, taaki bike ko car ke spots scan na karne padein.

Free-pool design

Idea: har spot type ke liye ek free spots ka queue maintain karo. Allocation = queue se poll(). Release = queue me wapas offer(). Dono O(1), aur ConcurrentLinkedQueue.poll() khud atomic hai.

ParkingFloor.java✓ O(1) claim
public class ParkingFloor {

    private final int floorNumber;
    private final Map<String, ParkingSpot> spotsById;
    private final Map<SpotType, Queue<ParkingSpot>> freeSpotsByType;
    private final Map<SpotType, AtomicInteger> freeCountByType;

    public ParkingFloor(int floorNumber, List<ParkingSpot> spots) {
        this.floorNumber = floorNumber;
        this.spotsById = spots.stream()
                .collect(Collectors.toUnmodifiableMap(
                        ParkingSpot::getSpotId, Function.identity()));

        this.freeSpotsByType = new EnumMap<>(SpotType.class);
        this.freeCountByType = new EnumMap<>(SpotType.class);
        for (SpotType type : SpotType.values()) {
            freeSpotsByType.put(type, new ConcurrentLinkedQueue<>());
            freeCountByType.put(type, new AtomicInteger(0));
        }
        spots.forEach(this::addToFreePool);
    }

    public Optional<ParkingSpot> claimFreeSpot(SpotType spotType, Vehicle vehicle) {
        Queue<ParkingSpot> pool = freeSpotsByType.get(spotType);
        ParkingSpot candidate;
        while ((candidate = pool.poll()) != null) {
            freeCountByType.get(spotType).decrementAndGet();
            if (candidate.tryOccupy(vehicle)) {
                return Optional.of(candidate);
            }
        }
        return Optional.empty();
    }

    public void releaseSpot(ParkingSpot spot) {
        spot.release();
        addToFreePool(spot);
    }

    private void addToFreePool(ParkingSpot spot) {
        freeSpotsByType.get(spot.getSpotType()).offer(spot);
        freeCountByType.get(spot.getSpotType()).incrementAndGet();
    }

    public int countFreeSpots(SpotType spotType) {
        return freeCountByType.get(spotType).get();
    }

    public int getFloorNumber() { return floorNumber; }
}

public class ParkingLotService {

    private final ParkingLot parkingLot;
    private final SpotAllocationStrategy allocationStrategy;
    private final PricingStrategy pricingStrategy;
    private final TicketRepository ticketRepository;
    private final Clock clock;

    public ParkingLotService(ParkingLot parkingLot,
                             SpotAllocationStrategy allocationStrategy,
                             PricingStrategy pricingStrategy,
                             TicketRepository ticketRepository,
                             Clock clock) {
        this.parkingLot = parkingLot;
        this.allocationStrategy = allocationStrategy;
        this.pricingStrategy = pricingStrategy;
        this.ticketRepository = ticketRepository;
        this.clock = clock;
    }

    public ParkingTicket issueTicketOnEntry(Vehicle vehicle, String entryGateId) {

        ParkingSpot spot = allocationStrategy
                .allocateSpot(parkingLot.getFloors(), vehicle)
                .orElseThrow(() -> new NoSpotAvailableException(vehicle.getVehicleType()));

        try {
            ParkingTicket ticket =
                    ParkingTicket.issueFor(spot, vehicle, entryGateId, clock);
            ticketRepository.save(ticket);
            return ticket;
        } catch (RuntimeException failure) {
            releaseSpotQuietly(spot);
            throw failure;
        }
    }

    public Money calculateExitFee(String ticketId) {
        ParkingTicket ticket = loadActiveTicket(ticketId);
        SpotType spotType = findSpotType(ticket.getSpotId());
        return pricingStrategy.calculateFee(ticket, spotType, Instant.now(clock));
    }

    public Receipt completeExit(String ticketId, PaymentMethod paymentMethod) {

        ParkingTicket ticket = loadActiveTicket(ticketId);
        Money fee = calculateExitFee(ticketId);

        PaymentResult result = paymentMethod.collect(fee, ticket.getTicketId());
        if (result.status() != PaymentStatus.SUCCESS) {
            throw new PaymentFailedException(ticketId, result.failureReason());
        }

        ticket.markPaid(fee);
        ticket.closeAfterExit(Instant.now(clock));
        ticketRepository.save(ticket);

        releaseSpotQuietly(findSpot(ticket.getSpotId()));
        return new Receipt(ticket.getTicketId(), fee, result.transactionId());
    }

    private void releaseSpotQuietly(ParkingSpot spot) {
        parkingLot.getFloors().stream()
                .filter(floor -> floor.getFloorNumber() == spot.getFloorNumber())
                .findFirst()
                .ifPresent(floor -> floor.releaseSpot(spot));
    }
}

public class HourlySlabPricing implements PricingStrategy {

    private final Map<SpotType, List<RateSlab>> slabsBySpotType;
    private final Duration freeGracePeriod;

    public HourlySlabPricing(Map<SpotType, List<RateSlab>> slabsBySpotType,
                             Duration freeGracePeriod) {
        this.slabsBySpotType = Map.copyOf(slabsBySpotType);
        this.freeGracePeriod = freeGracePeriod;
    }

    @Override
    public Money calculateFee(ParkingTicket ticket, SpotType spotType, Instant exitTime) {

        Duration parkedDuration = ticket.getParkedDuration(exitTime);

        if (parkedDuration.compareTo(freeGracePeriod) <= 0) {
            return Money.ZERO;
        }

        long billableHours = roundUpToWholeHours(parkedDuration);

        return slabsBySpotType.getOrDefault(spotType, List.of()).stream()
                .map(slab -> slab.ratePerHour()
                        .multipliedBy(slab.billableHoursWithin(billableHours)))
                .reduce(Money.ZERO, Money::plus);
    }

    private long roundUpToWholeHours(Duration duration) {
        return (duration.toMinutes() + 59) / 60;
    }
}

    public SurgePricingDecorator(PricingStrategy delegate,
                                BigDecimal surgeMultiplier,
                                Predicate<Instant> surgeWindow) {
        this.delegate = delegate;
        this.surgeMultiplier = surgeMultiplier;
        this.surgeWindow = surgeWindow;
    }

    @Override
    public Money calculateFee(ParkingTicket ticket, SpotType spotType, Instant exitTime) {

        Money baseFee = delegate.calculateFee(ticket, spotType, exitTime);

        if (!surgeWindow.test(ticket.getEntryTime())) {
            return baseFee;
        }
        return baseFee.multipliedBy(surgeMultiplier);
    }
}

Payments — idempotency hi asli sawaal hai

SDE-2SDE-3

Problem

Payment ka interface likhna aasan hai. Asli sawaal ye hai:

THE SCENARIO Exit gate ne payment gateway ko call kiya. Paisa cut gaya. Lekin response aate waqt network timeout ho gaya. Gate ko pata hi nahi chala ki success hua ya fail.

Ab gate retry karega. Kya customer ka paisa do baar katega?

Ye sirf theory nahi hai

Distributed systems me timeout ≠ failure. Timeout ka matlab sirf itna hai ki “mujhe pata nahi”. Request pahunchi bhi ho sakti hai, process bhi ho chuki ho sakti hai — sirf jawaab kho gaya.

Solution ka naam

Idempotency: wahi operation N baar chalao, result wahi rahega jo 1 baar chalane pe hota. Har payment request ke saath ek unique key bhejo. Gateway us key ko yaad rakhta hai.

UpiPayment.javaidempotent implementation
public class UpiPayment implements PaymentMethod {

    private final PaymentGatewayClient gatewayClient;
    private final PaymentLedgerRepository ledgerRepository;

    public UpiPayment(PaymentGatewayClient gatewayClient,
                      PaymentLedgerRepository ledgerRepository) {
        this.gatewayClient = gatewayClient;
        this.ledgerRepository = ledgerRepository;
    }

    @Override
    public PaymentResult collect(Money amount, String idempotencyKey) {

        Optional<PaymentRecord> existing =
                ledgerRepository.findByIdempotencyKey(idempotencyKey);
        if (existing.isPresent()) {
            return existing.get().toResult();
        }

        ledgerRepository.saveAttempt(idempotencyKey, amount, PaymentStatus.PENDING);

        try {
            GatewayResponse response =
                    gatewayClient.charge(amount, idempotencyKey);

            PaymentResult result = response.isApproved()
                    ? PaymentResult.success(response.transactionId())
                    : PaymentResult.failed(response.declineReason());

            ledgerRepository.finalise(idempotencyKey, result);
            return result;

        } catch (GatewayTimeoutException timeout) {
            return PaymentResult.pendingReconciliation(idempotencyKey);
        }
    }
}

Scale — 1 lot se 1000 lots tak

SDE-3

Problem

“Ab isko India ke 1000 malls ke liye scale karo. 5 lakh spots. Kya badlega?”

Pehle numbers nikalo

  • 1000 lots × 500 spots = 5,00,000 spots.
  • 4 turnovers/din → 20 lakh transactions/din.
  • Peak hour 15% → 3 lakh/hour → ~83 writes/sec.
  • Availability reads: har lot pe 50 clients × 0.2 rps = ~10,000 rps.
ASLI INSIGHT 83 writes/sec ek single Postgres instance aaram se handle kar leta hai. Ye scale problem nahi hai, ye availability aur blast-radius problem hai. Agar tum yahan sharding aur Cassandra ki baat karne lago to interviewer samjhega ki tumne numbers calculate kiye hi nahi.

Toh asli challenges kya hain?

  1. Ek lot ka outage baaki 999 lots ko na le doobe.
  2. Network cut hone pe gate kaam karta rahe.
  3. 10,000 rps read load DB pe na pade.
  4. Rate cards 1000 lots pe alag-alag hain — sab manage kaise ho.

1. Partition by lot_id — cross-shard koi transaction nahi

Ek parking spot hamesha ek hi lot ka hota hai. Do lots ke beech koi transaction hoti hi nahi. Isliye lot_id perfect shard key hai:

  • Har shard pe ~100 lots. 10 shards total.
  • Ek shard down → sirf 100 lots affected, 900 chalte rahenge.
  • Koi distributed transaction nahi — koi 2PC ki zaroorat hi nahi.

  • Dispute aaye to exact purana card se recalculate ho jaata hai.

Har question pe click karke answer kholo. Pehle khud jawaab socho, phir kholo — warna sirf padhna ho jaayega, practice nahi. Answers wahi hain jo actual interview me bolne layak hain: pehle direct jawaab, phir reason, phir trade-off.

SDE-1 — fundamentals aur OOP

01Vehicle ko abstract class banaya ya interface? Kyun?

Abstract class. Kyunki har vehicle ke paas shared state hai — registrationNumber aur vehicleType. Interface me state nahi rakh sakte (sirf constants), to har subclass me wahi do fields duplicate karne padte.

Rule: shared state chahiye → abstract class. Sirf shared contract chahiye → interface.

Trade-off jo bolna hai: “Java me single inheritance hai, to agar aage Vehicle ko kisi aur hierarchy me bhi fit karna pada to problem hogi. Us case me main composition use karunga — Vehicle ek concrete class ho aur behaviours alag interfaces se aayein.”

02“Kya gaadi is spot me fit hogi” ka logic Vehicle me rakhoge ya ParkingSpot me?

ParkingSpot me — spot.canFitVehicle(vehicle).

Reason: capacity spot ka property hai. Ek Honda City ko nahi pata ki mall ka stall kitna chauda hai. Knowledge wahan rakho jahan wo naturally hai.

Practical test: naya SpotType.OVERSIZED add karo. Spot pe logic hai to sirf SpotType enum badlega. Vehicle pe hota to har Vehicle subclass me naya check add karna padta — 5 files badalti.

03Optional return kyun kiya, null kyun nahi?

null return karo to caller check karna bhool sakta hai — compiler kuch nahi bolega, aur NPE production me milega. Optional me caller ko orElseThrow, ifPresent ya map me se kuch to likhna hi padega.

Ye bhi bolo: “Optional ka sahi use return type me hai. Fields aur method parameters me main isko avoid karta hoon — wo serialization aur readability dono kharab karta hai.”

041000 spots me se free spot O(1) me kaise dhoondhoge?

Free spots ka pool maintain karo, har spot type ke liye alag queue: Map<SpotType, Queue<ParkingSpot>>.

  • Allocate = queue.poll() → O(1)
  • Release = queue.offer(spot) → O(1)

Scan wala approach O(n) hai aur lot 95% bhara hone pe worst case hit karta hai — matlab jab load sabse zyada hai tabhi sabse slow hoga.

Bonus: ConcurrentLinkedQueue use karoge to poll() khud atomic hai, alag lock nahi chahiye.

05Vehicle me equals/hashCode override kyun kiya?

Kyunki vehicle ki business identity registration number hai, object reference nahi. DB se load karoge to naya object banega — default equals (reference comparison) us par false dega, jo galat hai.

Aur agar sirf equals override karo, hashCode nahi? Phir HashMap me vehicle daalke wapas nikalna kabhi kaam nahi karega — alag bucket me dhoondhega. Ye silent bug hai, koi exception nahi aata.

06Paise ke liye double kyun nahi?

double binary floating point hai. 0.1 + 0.2 ka answer 0.30000000000000004 aata hai. 10,000 transactions ke baad accounts reconcile nahi honge.

Sahi options: BigDecimal with explicit RoundingMode, ya long me minor units (paise). DB me NUMERIC(10,2), kabhi FLOAT nahi.

Extra point: ek Money value object banao. Isse rounding ek hi jagah hoti hai aur fee + tax jaisa code type-safe rehta hai.

SDE-2 — concurrency, extensibility, failure handling

07Do gates, ek hi last spot, dono pe ek saath gaadi. Kya hoga?

Naive code me dono ko wahi spot allot ho jaayega — check-then-act race condition. Dono isFree() pe true dekhte hain, dono park() call karte hain.

Fix: check aur act ko ek atomic operation banao:

  • Single JVM: AtomicBoolean.compareAndSet(false, true)
  • Multi-server: UPDATE spot SET status='OCCUPIED' WHERE id=? AND status='FREE' aur rowsAffected == 1 check

API-level fix (ye bolo, impress karta hai): public interface se isFree() hata do. Sirf tryOccupy() expose karo. Agar caller check hi nahi kar sakta, to race likhna structurally impossible ho jaata hai.

08synchronized, ReentrantLock aur AtomicBoolean me kya choose karoge?

AtomicBoolean, kyunki humein sirf ek boolean flag pe atomic transition chahiye — multi-step critical section nahi.

OptionKabCost
AtomicBooleanSingle variable CASLock-free, no blocking
synchronizedMulti-step critical sectionThread block hota hai; poora floor lock ho jaata
ReentrantLockTimeout ya interruptible lock chahiyeManual unlock — finally bhoolna bug hai

Key line: “synchronized se bhi correctness milti, lekin granularity poore object pe hoti. CAS spot-level granularity deta hai bina 1000 lock objects banaye.”

09Spot claim ho gaya, phir ticket save karte waqt DB down ho gaya. Ab?

Spot OCCUPIED marked hai lekin koi gaadi khadi nahi — orphan spot. Permanently lost inventory. 3 mahine me lot me “ghost” spots bhar jaayenge.

Do solutions, situation ke hisaab se:

  • In-memory: try/catch me compensating action — exception aaye to releaseSpotQuietly(spot), phir exception rethrow karo.
  • DB-backed (behtar): spot claim aur ticket insert ek hi transaction me. @Transactional lagao — rollback automatic ho jaayega, manual cleanup code chahiye hi nahi.

Safety net: ek background job jo aise spots dhoondhe jo OCCUPIED hain par unka koi ACTIVE ticket nahi — alert karo. Compensating logic me bhi bug ho sakta hai.

10Weekend pe 1.5× surge chahiye. Kitna code badlega?

Zero existing code. Ek naya class add hoga: SurgePricingDecorator implements PricingStrategy, jo kisi bhi doosri strategy ko wrap kar leta hai.

Wiring aisi hogi:
new SurgePricingDecorator(new HourlySlabPricing(rateCard), new BigDecimal("1.5"), isWeekend)

Decorator kyun, seedha if kyun nahi? Kyunki kal loyalty discount aur GST bhi aayenge. Inheritance se karoge to har combination ki subclass banegi — 3 rules = 8 classes. Decorator se 3 classes, aur runtime pe koi bhi combination bana sakte ho.

Ek detail jo poochhi jaayegi: surge entryTime pe decide hota hai, exitTime pe nahi. Driver ko andar ghuste waqt rate pata hona chahiye.

11Truck ko 2 contiguous spots chahiye. Design kaise badlega?

Ye fundamental change hai, cosmetic nahi — aur ye bolna hi sahi answer ki shuruaat hai.

  • Spot pe position chahiye: rowNumber + positionInRow, taaki adjacency define ho sake.
  • Free pool kaam nahi karega: queue random order deta hai, adjacency preserve nahi karta. Har row ke liye occupancy bitmap chahiye.
  • Allocation ab search hai: bitmap me k consecutive zeroes dhoondho — sliding window, O(spots per row).
  • Claim ab multi-spot atomic hona chahiye: ek CAS kaafi nahi. Row-level lock lo ya DB me single UPDATE se saare k spots claim karo (WHERE spot_id IN (...) AND status='FREE') aur rowsAffected == k verify karo. Partial claim = rollback.
  • Fragmentation naya problem hai: chhote vehicles bikhar ke park honge to 2 contiguous kabhi nahi milenge. Isliye trucks ke liye dedicated zone rakhna practically better hai.

Recommendation: “Main pehle poochhunga ki kitne % trucks hain. Agar 5% hain to dedicated truck zone banana bahut simpler aur reliable hai contiguous allocation ke muqable.”

12Customer ka ticket kho gaya. Kya karoge?

Flow:

  1. Vehicle number se lookup: WHERE vehicle_number=? AND status='ACTIVE'. Isiliye ticket table me vehicle number store kiya tha.
  2. Identity verify karo — RC book, ya entry camera ki photo se match.
  3. Fee: actual entryTime se, plus lost-ticket penalty.
  4. Ticket status LOST mark karo (alag se, taaki audit me dikhe).
  5. Payment, phir spot release.

Entry record hi na mile to? Policy-driven maximum daily rate charge karo. Ye deliberate hai — warna log ticket “kho” jaane ka bahana banake sasta nikal jaayenge.

Design lesson: ticket ek convenience hona chahiye, source of truth nahi. Source of truth server-side record hai.

13Singleton use kiya — iske problems kya hain?

Haan, problems hain, aur main inhe jaanta hoon:

  • Testing: global state tests ke beech leak karta hai. Har test me reset karne ka hack likhna padta hai.
  • Hidden dependency: ParkingLot.getInstance() class ke andar se call hoti hai, isliye signature dekhke pata hi nahi chalta ki ye class lot pe depend karti hai.
  • Multi-lot pe tootta hai: jis din doosra lot aayega, poora pattern badalna padega.
  • Lazy init thread-safety: volatile + double-checked locking karna padta hai, warna partially-constructed object leak ho sakta hai.

Main kya karunga: “Interview me main singleton dikha raha hoon kyunki expected hai, lekin production me main ise Spring bean banaunga aur constructor se inject karunga. Uniqueness DI container guarantee karega, class khud nahi — aur test me alag instance bana sakta hoon.”

14Fee calculation me sabse common bug kya hai?

duration.toHours() use karna. Java me ye truncate karta hai: 59 minute = 0 hours = ₹0 fee. Parking hamesha round up karti hai.

Fix: (duration.toMinutes() + 59) / 60. Integer division neeche truncate karta hai, isliye pehle 59 add karke effectively ceiling ban jaata hai. 61 min → (61+59)/60 = 2 hours.

Doosra common bug: LocalDateTime use karna Instant/TIMESTAMPTZ ki jagah. DST switch wali raat me customer ko ek ghanta free parking mil jaayega, ya double charge ho jaayega.

SDE-3 — distributed systems aur failure modes

153 app servers load balancer ke peeche hain. AtomicBoolean kaam karega?

Nahi. AtomicBoolean CPU ka CAS instruction use karta hai — wo ek process ki memory tak seemit hai. Teen JVMs ke paas teen alag ParkingSpot objects honge, teen alag flags. Sab claim “jeet” jaayenge.

Sahi approach: shared source of truth ko arbitrator banao.

  • DB conditional update (preferred): UPDATE ... WHERE status='FREE' + rowsAffected==1. Ek round trip, no extra infra.
  • Redis lock (agar DB option na ho): SET lock:spot:{id} {token} NX PX 5000. Fencing token zaroori hai — warna expired lock holder purana write kar dega.

In-memory cache ab kya hai? Sirf ek hint. Usse candidate spot chuno, par authoritative claim hamesha DB pe karo.

16Payment gateway ko call kiya, paisa cut gaya, par response timeout ho gaya. Retry karoge?

Retry safe hai — lekin sirf tab jab idempotency key ho.

Key insight: distributed systems me timeout ≠ failure. Timeout ka matlab sirf “mujhe pata nahi” hai. Request process ho chuki ho sakti hai, sirf response kho gaya.

Implementation:

  • Har payment attempt ke saath unique idempotencyKey (yahan ticketId + attempt).
  • Gateway call se pehle apne ledger me PENDING row likho.
  • Wahi key gateway ko bhi bhejo — wo bhi dedup karega.
  • Retry pe pehle ledger check karo; already record hai to gateway ko call hi mat karo.

Sabse important: timeout pe status FAILED mat likho — PENDING rakho aur reconciliation job se resolve karo. Timeout ko FAILED maanne ka matlab hai: customer ka paisa kat gaya aur system keh raha hai “payment nahi hua”.

Practical business call: driver ko 5 min gate pe rok nahi sakte. PENDING pe bhi barrier khol do, peeche se reconcile karo. ₹50 ka risk 20 gaadiyon ki line se sasta hai.

17Kafka event ko transaction ke andar publish karna theek hai?

Nahi. Ye classic dual write problem hai. DB commit aur Kafka publish do alag systems hain — inko atomically nahi kar sakte.

Do failure cases:

  • Event publish ho gaya, phir txn rollback → availability counter hamesha ke liye galat.
  • Txn commit ho gaya, phir publish fail → counter update hi nahi hua.

Solution: transactional outbox pattern.

  1. Business transaction ke andar ek outbox_event row insert karo. Same DB, same commit — atomicity guaranteed.
  2. Alag poller (ya CDC/Debezium) WHERE published_at IS NULL se rows uthata hai aur Kafka pe daalta hai.
  3. Publish ke baad published_at set kar deta hai.

Delivery semantics: ye at-least-once deta hai — poller crash hone pe duplicate ja sakta hai. Isliye consumers ko idempotent banao (event_id pe dedup), aur counters ke liye periodic reconciliation job rakho.

181000 lots, 5 lakh spots. Sharding kaise karoge?

Pehle numbers: 20 lakh transactions/din, peak ~83 writes/sec. Ye ek single Postgres instance ke liye kuch bhi nahi.

To ye scale problem nahi hai — ye blast-radius problem hai. Main partition isolation ke liye karunga, throughput ke liye nahi.

  • Shard key: lot_id. Ek spot hamesha ek lot ka hota hai. Do lots ke beech koi transaction hoti hi nahi — matlab zero cross-shard transactions, koi 2PC nahi.
  • 10 shards, har shard pe ~100 lots. Ek shard down = 900 lots normal chalte rahenge.
  • Reads (10,000 rps availability) Redis se, DB se bilkul nahi.

Ye line bolo: “Parking embarrassingly partitionable hai kyunki physical isolation already exist karta hai. Ye rare luxury hai — zyadatar systems me shard boundaries artificial hoti hain aur cross-shard queries dard dete hain. Yahan nahi.”

19Gate ka network 2 ghante ke liye chala gaya. Parking band kar denge?

Bilkul nahi. Gate ko autonomously chalna chahiye.

  • Spot block pre-allocation: har gate ko 20 spots ka reserved block pehle se allot rakho. Offline hone pe gate apne block se allot karega — conflict structurally impossible hai kyunki wo block kisi aur gate ke paas hai hi nahi.
  • Client-generated ticket ID: UUID gate banata hai. Server pe baad me upsert hoga. Ticket printing server pe depend nahi karti.
  • Local write-ahead log: SQLite me entries likho, network aane pe replay.
  • Exit degraded mode: cached rate card se fee nikaalo, payment offline queue me daalo, barrier khol do. Reconcile baad me.
  • Cash fallback: card/UPI down ho to cash accept karo — attendant manually confirm kare.

Philosophy: “Physical infrastructure ko network pe hard-depend nahi karna chahiye. Gate barrier ek mechanical device hai — uska default behaviour ‘degraded but functional’ hona chahiye, ‘dead’ nahi.”

20Advance booking / reservation feature add karna hai. Kya badlega?

1. Naya spot state: FREE → RESERVED → OCCUPIED. RESERVED spot allocation pool me nahi aana chahiye.

2. Reservation entity: reservationId, spotId, vehicleNumber, validFrom, validUntil, status.

3. Expiry job (yahi asli problem hai): banda aaya hi nahi to spot hamesha ke liye block nahi hona chahiye. Ek scheduled job validUntil < now AND status='RESERVED' wale spots ko release kare. Grace period 15 min rakho.

4. Inventory split: ab do pools hain — reserved aur walk-in. Business decide karega ki kitne % reserve ho sakte hain. 100% reservable karoge to walk-in customers ko hamesha “FULL” dikhega, chahe aadha lot khaali ho.

5. Overbooking (advanced): airlines jaisa — 10% no-show rate assume karke thoda over-book karo. Utilization badhta hai lekin ek fallback policy chahiye (nearest lot me free parking + refund).

6. Pricing badlega: reservation pe advance payment ya hold amount, plus cancellation policy.

Bolne wali line: “Reservation sabse zyada asar allocation strategy pe daalta hai — ab wo sirf ‘free spot dhoondho’ nahi hai, balki ‘free spot dhoondho jo kisi upcoming reservation ke liye block nahi hai’ ban jaata hai. Ye time-aware allocation hai, aur yahi is feature ka asli complexity hai.”

INTERVIEW KE AAKHIR ME YE BOLO “Agar mere paas 10 minute aur hote, to main teen cheezein aur cover karta: lost-ticket recovery ka detailed flow, spot allocation ke liye ek load test ka plan, aur reservation ke saath walk-in inventory ka balance. In teeno pe mere paas approach hai — aap batayein kaunsa dekhna chahenge?”

Ye aakhri line bahut kaam karti hai: dikhati hai ki tumhe apne design ki boundaries pata hain, aur control interviewer ko wapas de deti hai.
